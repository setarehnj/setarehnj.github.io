Mathematics of Alignment

Mathematical questions about agents, preferences, oversight, and learned behavior.

This is a research-oriented collection of mathematical explorations motivated by concrete problems in AI alignment. Rather than equating advanced mathematics with alignment, each note will state its alignment question, the mathematical assumptions, and what the results do—and do not—establish.

For background courses in analysis, probability, optimization, and geometry, see Math for ML.

Agency and decision-making

Decision theory and agent foundations

Preference representation, expected utility, sequential decisions, and formal models of rational agency.

Game theory and multi-agent systems

Strategic interaction, mechanism design, and models of cooperation and conflict.

Learning and oversight

Preference learning and reward misspecification

Inference from imperfect feedback, inverse problems, uncertainty over preferences, and objective misspecification.

Calibration, robustness, and evaluations

Statistical guarantees, online learning, distribution shift, and the limits of oversight.

Representations and training dynamics

Information theory and interpretability

Representational structure, information flow, and the mathematical limits of what observations reveal about learned computations.

Singular learning theory

Singular statistical models, Bayesian asymptotics, and carefully stated hypotheses about neural-network learning dynamics.

Research notes

Each note will begin with a precise question and distinguish established theorems from conjectures and speculative applications.
